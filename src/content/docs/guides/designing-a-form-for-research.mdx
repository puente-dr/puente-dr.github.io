A research file is only as good as the form behind it. Every choice in this guide is cheap before the clinic and impossible to fix after it. Use it when a research partner will receive data from a form you build in Manage.

When you build a form, you are really designing a spreadsheet. Each question becomes a column, with your question's words as its heading. Each time someone fills in the form, that becomes a row.

| In the form | In the research file |
|---|---|
| a question: *Acuity, right eye, without glasses* | a column with that heading |
| one patient's visit, filled in | one row |
| the answer chosen: *20/200* | the value in that cell |

## Start from the columns

Ask the research team, in writing, for the list of columns they need. For a vision study it might be: consent; vision in each eye before glasses; the prescription measured; the glasses given; vision in each eye with the glasses.

{/* claim: guide-research-columns */}
Some things you do not need to ask. The research file brings in age, sex, community, city and province from the patient's record, next to a study ID for the patient.

## One form per visit

Put everything measured at one visit into one form: the test before, what was given, the test after. Then each row of the file is one complete visit.

If you split a visit across two forms, someone later has to match a row in one file to a row in another, and every mismatch is a patient lost from the study.

## Pick from a list; don't type

{/* claim: guide-seven-blocks */}
The Form Creator offers seven blocks. Two matter most for research:

| Use | For | Example |
|---|---|---|
| <UI>Question - Single select</UI> | anything with standard values | a vision line (20/40), a lens power (−2.25), yes or no |
| <UI>Question - Number response</UI> | true measurements with no fixed list | a lens axis between 0 and 180 |

On a busy day a typed number can lose its minus sign or its decimal point, and nothing in the file shows that it happened. A list cannot.

<Shot src={blocks} alt="The Blocks panel in the Form Creator listing the seven kinds of question." caption="The seven blocks. For research, use Single select wherever the answers come from a known list." />

## Community from a list

{/* claim: guide-community-spellings */}
The community on a patient's record is entered in Collect, and the same place often ends up spelled several ways: `El Río`, `El Rio`. To a spreadsheet those are different places. If the study groups patients by community, add a single-select question listing the communities the clinic serves, spelled once, plus "Other".

## Ask for consent first

Make the first question a single select: has the patient agreed to their data being used for this study? Copy the wording from the ethics approval word for word.

<Aside type="caution" title="The file includes the patients who said no">
{/* claim: guide-research-all-rows */}
The research file has a row for every time the form was filled in, whatever the consent answer. Those rows are removed before the file is sent; [Sharing data with researchers](/docs/guides/sharing-data-with-researchers/) shows how.
</Aside>

## Nothing that identifies anyone

Don't ask for names, phone numbers or ID numbers inside the form. They are already on the patient's record, and the research file leaves the record's copies out. A question that asks for one would put it straight back in.

{/* claim: guide-research-geolocation-out */}
A <UI>Geolocation</UI> question is the exception: the research file leaves location questions out on its own.

## Turn on the research file

{/* claim: guide-research-opt-in */}
Every form starts with the research file turned off. Someone has to decide, on purpose, that this form's answers may leave Puente.

1. In Manage, open <UI>Create</UI>, or open your form with <UI>Edit</UI>.
2. Under the name and description, tick <UI>Allow research export</UI>. Read the sentence below it.
3. Press <UI>Publish</UI>.

<Shot src={creator} alt="The Form Creator with Type of form, Form name and Description filled in, an Allow research export checkbox with its explanation below, and the Blocks panel on the right." caption="The sentence under the checkbox says exactly what turning it on adds." />

## Test it with a copy

{/* claim: guide-test-on-copy */}
Test patients sent to the real form would end up in the real research file. Use a copy instead.

1. In Form Manager, press <UI>Duplicate</UI> on the form.
2. Start the copy's name with `PRUEBA -` so nobody mistakes it for the real one, tick <UI>Allow research export</UI>, and publish.
3. On a phone, fill in the copy for two or three invented patients, and sync.
4. Press <UI>Export research file</UI> on the copy. Open the file and read every column heading.
5. Send that test file to the research team and ask them to confirm it has what they need.
6. When they say yes, delete the copy.

<Shot src={formManager} alt="Form Manager with custom forms listed, each with Edit, Duplicate, Delete and Export, and the Vision exam form marked Research export on with an Export research file button." caption="Duplicate makes the test copy; delete it when the test is done." />

{/* claim: guide-publish-silent */}
Manage does not show an error if publishing fails. After publishing, check that the form appears on a phone under <UI>Custom Forms</UI>.

## FAQ

{/* claim: guide-research-checked-on-export */}
**Can I turn the research file on after the clinic?**
Yes. The setting is checked when someone presses the export button, so the file includes visits collected before you turned it on.

{/* claim: guide-accents-stripped */}
**Why are the accents missing in the headings?**
Custom-form exports remove accents from headings and answers. Write questions you can still recognize without them.
